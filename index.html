Room Observations & Constraints

Bright Yellow Vinyl Silk

Issue: Vinyl silk is shiny and slightly flexible. Standard matt emulsion may slide off, cure poorly, or scratch off if applied directly.

Action required: Lightly sand the walls to remove the gloss, clean gently, and apply a bonding primer before the pale green or aubergine matt topcoats.

Bubbling Woodchip

Issue: Approx. 1 m² of old woodchip wallpaper is bubbling near the bottom on the front/radiator-side external wall area.

Action required: Treat it as a small localised repair, not a reason to strip the whole room or plaster. Check it is dry and stable before sealing or painting.

Bespoke Preparation & Repair Methods

1. Repairing the Bubbled Woodchip

Because the bubbled area is only around 1 m², this should be treated as a small localised repair. Do not strip the whole room, plaster the wall, or use a mist coat unless bare plaster is exposed.

  • Adhesive: Use proper wallpaper seam adhesive or wallpaper repair adhesive where the paper can be safely re-bonded.
  • Inject or brush: Use a small brush, nozzle, or syringe to get adhesive behind the bubble if possible.
  • Press and wipe: Gently press the paper flat with a clean sponge or seam roller, then wipe away excess adhesive immediately.
  • If it will not sit flat: Carefully cut away only the failed loose section with a sharp knife and seal the exposed edges.
  • Fill and texture: Fill small damaged areas with Toupret-style interior filler and lightly stipple the wet filler with a damp sponge to mimic woodchip texture.
  • Prime: Prime the repaired patch before painting.

Would PVA Glue Work?

PVA might physically stick a small loose wallpaper area down, but it is not the best first-choice repair here. It can seal the surface, create shiny patches, affect future paint adhesion, make later repair harder, show through paint, and fail again if condensation is involved.

Practical note: If bubbling is caused by ongoing cold-wall condensation, the paint may fail again unless ventilation is managed.

2. Preparing the Vinyl Silk Walls

  • De-gloss: Lightly sand the entire wall surface with medium-fine sandpaper to dull the shiny silk finish.
  • Clean: Wash gently with sugar soap to remove sanding dust and grease. Do not heavily soak wallpaper areas.
  • Bonding primer: Apply one full coat of high-adhesion bonding primer, such as Zinsser Bulls Eye 1-2-3.
  • Topcoat: Apply two coats of trade vinyl matt emulsion.

3. Filling Old Picture Holes

Rake out loose debris, over-fill slightly with Toupret-style interior filler, allow to dry fully, sand flat with a sanding block, then spot-prime before the main wall coats.

Step-by-Step Decorating Plan

1

Clear and Protect

Move furniture to the centre of the room. Lay dust sheets across the floor space. Mask off skirting boards, the north wall entry doorway frame, the west wall radiator and light switches.

Tools: Dust sheets, masking tape Active time: 1 hour
2

De-gloss the Vinyl Silk

Lightly sand the bright yellow walls with a medium-fine sanding block to break the shiny surface. Wash down with sugar soap and a sponge, rinse with clean water, and allow to dry.

Tools: Sanding block, sugar soap, PPE Active time: 2 hours Drying time: 1–2 hours
3

Localised Woodchip Repair and Fill Holes

Inject or brush proper seam adhesive behind the 1 m² bubbled woodchip near the radiator and press flat. If it will not flatten, cut away the loose section, seal edges, fill the void with Toupret-style filler, and stipple with a damp sponge to mimic texture. Fill old picture holes flush.

Tools: Seam adhesive, craft knife, filler, sponge Active time: 1.5 hours Drying time: overnight safest
4

Prime Surfaces

Sand the picture-hole filler smooth while wearing a mask. Spot-prime repairs and filled holes, then apply one full coat of Zinsser Bulls Eye 1-2-3 bonding primer over all yellow vinyl silk walls.

Tools: Roller, angled brush, primer, mask Active time: 2.5 hours Drying time: 2–6 hours, check tin
5

Cut In and Roll Main Walls

Using the pale green trade vinyl matt, cut in around the ceiling line, skirting, bay window framing and west wall radiator. Roll the main walls without overloading the roller.

Tools: Angled brush, roller, pale green paint Active time: 2–3 hours
6

Cut In and Roll Alcoves

Cut in and roll the 1.29 m north alcove and 1.20 m south alcove on the east wall using aubergine matt. Apply painter’s tape carefully along the chimney breast return edge for a sharp line.

Tools: Clean brush, clean roller, tape, aubergine paint Active time: 1.5 hours
7

Apply Second Coats

Once the first coats are fully dry according to the manufacturer’s schedule, repeat the pale green and aubergine coats for a solid, opaque finish that hides the old yellow.

Active time: 3–4 hours Drying time: 2–4 hours per coat

Big Tip: Safety & Masking

  • Ventilate: Always ventilate the room well when sanding, priming and painting.
  • PPE: Wear eye protection and a suitable mask when sanding the vinyl silk and dried filler.
  • Tape removal: Remove masking tape while the final coat is slightly damp to prevent peeling fresh paint.
  • Radiator access: Protect the floor beneath the west wall radiator and use a mini roller on a long handle to reach behind it.

Final Finish Checklist

Before Painting

  • Bubbled woodchip safely re-adhered or trimmed back.
  • Repaired areas filled and stippled to match texture.
  • Picture holes filled and sanded completely smooth.
  • Dust removed from all walls and skirting.
  • Vinyl silk walls entirely coated in bonding primer.

After Painting

  • Edges masked securely, especially the chimney and alcove split.
  • First coat applied and fully dried.
  • Second coat applied for opaque finish.
  • Tape removed carefully while paint is slightly damp.
  • Leftover aubergine and pale green paint labelled for future touch-ups.
